                          How this was computed

                          • Post-game only. This analyzes finished replays. It never runs alongside a live battle: live inference would be real-time assistance. Beliefs at each turn use only what happened before it.
                          • What is certain. Stat-point bounds - from the exact Showdown damage formula (0 mismatches vs the simulator over 6,800 cases, plus 360 for the move variants this report handles), the Champions HP display floor(100*hp/maxhp), turn order, and fixed-fraction HP changes (Leftovers, Life Orb, Sitrus Berry, ...) - and the solver's guaranteed win probabilities (a lower bound against every opponent play, with every unknown resolved against the player). A hit, turn order or HP change the calc can't reproduce exactly is skipped, never approximated, and listed under "Not analyzable here". KO counts are given for the least and most favourable spreads the bounds allow ("KOs in 9-16/16 rolls depending on the spreads") and are sound: damage is monotone in each stat. A turn's calcs use what was known at the start of that turn; "with everything the set revealed" redoes them with the end-of-set bounds. A key moment (a KO or a survival on a roll) is listed only when it is certain with the end-of-set bounds.
                          • HP trajectories. The display only shows floor(100*hp/maxhp), but the HP between two changes is one number: a Pokemon at 100% has exactly its max HP, Life Orb then takes exactly 1/10 of it, Grassy Terrain gives back 1/16, a hit takes one of its damage rolls, recoil and drain are a rounded fraction of the HP the target lost. Each Pokemon's exact HP is followed through the game as the set of (max HP, HP) pairs still possible; a change not modeled exactly keeps only the new shown %, and switching out forgets the exact HP. Max HP values that no pair survives are ruled out.
                          • A joint solve. One observation in a replay ties two unknown spreads together (the attacker's Attack to the defender's HP and Defense). Each Pokemon's whole spread is one variable; the observations between two Pokemon are joined into one exact table, and the bounds are propagated until nothing changes, with each Pokemon's side solved exactly (its HP and Defense together, the 66-point budget included). Sound: a value is dropped only if no spread of the other Pokemon allows it.
                          • What is an estimate. The particle posterior (prior: ladder-meta spreads (Smogon usage stats, by nature where the sheet shows it); reweighted by the same observations, averaged over the other Pokemon's belief when both spreads are unknown), reported conditioned on the bounds.
                          • Moves not used. What another move of the same Pokemon would have done to a foe at the moment it acted (its HP, the field), with the end-of-set bounds, assuming it hits. Listed only when it KOs in at least half the rolls for every spread the set allows, and never into a Pokemon that Protected that turn (or into Wide / Quick Guard).
                          • Solver. Runs from a pasted side at the start of each turn with at most 6 Pokemon left (depth 6 turns, 2000 ms per search, threshold 99%). "No forced win found" means none within the horizon, not a forced loss.
                          • Win path (estimates). A small model the endgame solver taught, chess-tablebase style: it was trained on the turn starts of thousands of recorded games (the trained policy against itself and a scripted player), labelled with what the solver proved from each side and who won. From each side's view it estimates the win chance and the chance of reaching a position the solver proves won, from the position the solver itself reads (HP, status, boosts, every pair's best damage and speed order, the field) - with the pasted spreads, or, without a paste, the estimated ones. The estimates are dashed lines and are never called a proof; "point of no return" is the first turn the solver proved a win (a proof); "where it slipped" is the biggest one-turn drop of the losing side's estimate (an estimate).
                          • What the bot would play (estimates). RNADy-Bot (checkpoint RNADy-Bot, knowledge explicit_d2v3) replays each viewed side's decisions: at every turn start it chooses as it plays (policy thresh:0.15 + search value2,depth=2: the engine's one-turn matrices, value-net leaves two turns deep, a regularized equilibrium against the opponent's replies). Its options are joint actions (both Pokemon's moves together), ranked by the search's value against the opponent's equilibrium replies; the move actually played is valued the same way (a move outside the options the bot searched is valued by searching again with it added to the candidates, so the opponent's replies account for it). Win estimates: a monotone (isotonic) map per game phase from the search score to the game outcome, fitted on the fit half of the calibration study (scripts/explain_calibration.py). Options within the noise band of the best (early 6 points, mid 7 points, late 8 points) are shown as "≈ best", not as separate numbers; a played move is marked only for a large drop (at least 20 points and 3 bands) or when it left a win the solver proved - never a "blunder" from a small difference. "≥99% (estimate)" is the bot's estimate; "guaranteed win" is only ever the solver's proof. Cost here: 1.7 s search + 0.4 s valuing the options per turn and side (54 decisions).
