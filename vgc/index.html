Project · AI for competitive Pokémon

An AI that plays competitive Pokémon, and explains its moves

I built a bot that plays Pokémon doubles, the format of the official world championships. I also built a tool that replays finished games turn by turn and shows what the bot would have done, with its estimated chance of winning.

Why this is harder than chess

In chess both players see everything and take turns. None of that holds here.

  • Both players move at the same time

    Blind

    It's like rock-paper-scissors: there's often no single best move, so the bot has to be hard to predict.

  • Two Pokémon per side

    2v2

    Every turn is two decisions that have to work together, against two of the opponent's.

  • Some information is hidden

    ?

    You see the opponent's team but not all of its numbers. You have to infer them from what happens, as in poker.

  • Luck is built in

    Dice

    Damage, accuracy and critical hits are random. A good decision can still lose, so the bot has to judge decisions, not results.

  • Matches are best of three

    Bo3

    The opponent learns from game one, and so must you.

  • Too big to solve by brute force

    10139

    There are about 10139 possible teams, far more than the number of atoms in the universe. The bot has to generalize.

How I built it

  • It first learned by copying top human players' games.
  • Then it improved by playing millions of games against itself, using a method DeepMind developed for strategy games with hidden information.
  • Before each move it thinks a couple of turns ahead.
  • In endgames it calculates exactly, and it only claims a win it has proven.
  • It was taught the rules of the game, never which moves are good.

Results so far

  • Beats a hand-coded expert bot

    8 in 10

    It wins 82-85% of best-of-three matches against two scripted bots that calculate damage exactly and use standard doubles tactics. That's 256 matches against each, played with teams real players used.

  • It keeps getting better

    72%

    The current version beats an earlier version of itself in 72% of matches.

  • Next: real people

    Soon

    It will play rated human players on the Pokémon Showdown ladder. Its rating will go here.

How the numbers are made

  • The bot. A transformer policy trained with R-NaD (DeepNash's algorithm) by self-play, starting from behaviour cloning on top players' replays. At each turn a small search ranks its candidate joint moves, both Pokémon together, against the opponent's likely replies.
  • Win estimates are calibrated. The raw search values were too extreme, so they were mapped to observed outcomes with an isotonic fit on 1,200 bot-vs-bot sets (about 16,000 decisions), then checked on held-out games: a shown percentage is off by 1.4 points on average, and by under 4 points in every tenth of the range. Options the bot did not choose, played on purpose, are calibrated too.
  • Small gaps are noise. Options within a few points of the best are marked "≈ best", using a band (6 to 8 points) measured by searching the same positions twice.
  • A human move outside the bot's options is valued by a second search with that move added, so the opponent's reply accounts for it.
  • Proofs and estimates are kept apart. The "proven" badge comes from an exact endgame solver; everything else is labelled as an estimate.
  • Each side's numbers use only that side's view of the game, so the two needn't add up to 100%.
