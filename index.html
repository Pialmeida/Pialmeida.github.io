01 · About

Physics meets data.

I'm finishing a PhD in Ocean Engineering (Applied Mathematics) at Florida Atlantic University, where I study turbulence with direct numerical simulation and teach reinforcement-learning agents to control fluid flow. Along the way I've shipped machine learning in industry, from an LLM-powered text-to-SQL system to churn and claims classifiers scored every week.

What ties it together is the part of engineering I enjoy most: taking a messy problem, finding the right abstraction, and building the tool that answers it, whether that's a crash-tolerant downloader for terabytes of DNS data, a C++ bridge between a CFD solver and an RL library, or a self-play reinforcement-learning agent for a side project.

Open to full-time ML, data science, CFD, and thermal engineering roles starting spring 2027.

03 · Projects

Selected work

Research · 2021–2024

Flow control with deep reinforcement learning

Coupled an OpenFOAM large-eddy simulation to the smarties RL library in C++, running 4 parallel simulations on 128 MPI ranks each. An off-policy agent (ReF-ER) reading 16 surface-pressure sensors learned to drive 4 rotating actuators and reduced cylinder drag by 70%, and the surface-pressure analysis identified the mechanism behind the separation control.

The simulation at the top of this page is a tiny, browser-sized cousin of this setup, with a heated plate added: lattice-Boltzmann flow plus a thermal lattice for temperature, reporting a live wall Nusselt number.

  • C++
  • OpenFOAM
  • Deep RL
  • MPI

Research · 2024–present

Wall-pressure extrema in wall-bounded turbulence

Built a parallel, crash-tolerant downloader and MATLAB/Python pipeline to process 6+ TB of JHTDB data, ran DNS of turbulent pipe flow with Nek5000, and characterized the shape, orientation, and dynamics of 50,000+ pressure extrema per case across channel, pipe, and boundary-layer flows.

  • Nek5000
  • Python
  • MATLAB
  • DNS

Computer vision · 2026

A Fourier fingerprint for eyewear frame shape

Segmented eyewear lens boundaries with Meta's Segment Anything Model, encoded the boundary points as complex numbers, and decomposed them via eigenpolygon (Fourier) analysis sorted by frequency magnitude — a compact numerical descriptor for frame style. Combined with CV-derived frame color (k-means matched to a perceptually-uniform palette), lens thickness, and lens type, it drove a prototype cold-start sales forecast that improved WMAPE by 2% at the SKU level and 8%/12% when aggregated by color and brand.

Built during my VSP Vision internship; presented to the team but not yet in production.

  • Computer vision
  • SAM
  • Fourier analysis
  • Forecasting

Personal · 2026–present

Self-play RL for competitive Pokémon

Implemented R-NaD (the algorithm behind DeepMind's DeepNash) from the papers, with a PPO baseline, a Transformer + GRU encoder, and a particle filter that infers hidden opponent information. An alpha-beta search engine bounds win probability under hidden information. Training runs on multi-node SLURM jobs past 1M self-play games per seed, backed by 1,800+ tests.

  • PyTorch
  • Game theory
  • HPC
